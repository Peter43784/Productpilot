The Problem

Six ways product decisions
go wrong every quarter

A PM at a 40-person SaaS has 6 hours of focus time per week, 400 unread support tickets, 80 G2 reviews, and a planning meeting on Friday. Something always gets missed.

pain — 01
📂

Fragmented & Un-synthesized Inputs

User signal lives across Zendesk, Notion, Slack, Gong, G2 and NPS exports. No PM reads half of it before writing a PRD. Decisions skew toward whichever ticket the CEO forwarded that morning.

→ features built for the loudest 5% of customers
pain — 02
📊

Opportunity Sizing by Feel

PRDs say “Impact: High / Effort: Medium.” RICE scores are fabricated post-hoc to justify decisions already made. Prioritization becomes political, not analytical.

→ roadmaps skew to whoever argues loudest
pain — 03
🧠

Zero Institutional Memory

Competitive scans done in Q1 are buried and re-done in Q3. Interview insights die in a Google Doc. New PMs take 6 months to build context an experienced PM has.

→ ~1 PM-week per team wasted each quarter
pain — 04
📝

PRD Quality Drifts Undetected

Teams have templates. Adherence varies wildly. Most PRDs say “improve user experience” where a success metric should be. Ambiguity is caught mid-sprint, not before.

→ 1–3 engineering days rework per ambiguous PRD
pain — 05
🔭

Stale Competitive Intelligence

Competitive scans happen once and go stale within weeks. Nobody refreshes because the effort is high. Differentiation claims in the PRD turn out to be wrong by the time engineering ships.

→ product bets made on a quarter-old picture
pain — 06
🔍

Invisible Reasoning Trail

When a decision gets challenged three months later, the reasoning is gone. Teams either refuse to revisit (sunk cost) or relitigate from scratch. Learning across quarters is structurally weak.

→ under 20% of past decisions auditable

Product Value

What ProductPilot delivers

Not a better PRD template. A consultant-grade system that reasons over your actual data, self-critiques its output, and leaves a trace you can stand behind six months later.

  • 🔄

    Multi-source synthesis

    Ingests Zendesk CSVs, Notion docs, call transcripts, G2 reviews, and NPS exports in one pass. Clusters themes with frequency counts, sentiment, and representative quotes.

  • 📐

    Sourced RICE scoring

    Opportunity sizing computed from theme cluster frequencies with confidence labels — not gut feel. Every number traces to a source theme with stated confidence.

  • 🏛

    Persistent org memory

    Every PRD, research synthesis, and competitive scan is written back to a RAG index. Decisions made in Q1 are instantly retrievable with citations in Q3.

  • ⚖

    Critic-loop quality gate

    A Critic agent scores every draft against a 7-point rubric. Below-threshold drafts return to Writer with structured feedback. Max 2 critic revisions + 2 synthesis revisions to cap cost and latency.

  • 🔬

    Full reasoning trace

    Every PRD ships with a trace bundle: sources consulted, themes extracted, RICE inputs, Critic/synthesis revision history. The “why did we build this?” has a real answer.

  • 🧑

    Human-in-the-loop checkpoints

    PM approves the research synthesis before the PRD is drafted. PM approves the final PRD before commit. Agents augment judgment — they don’t replace it.

Evidence It Works

10 scripted scenarios.
Real traces. Named failure modes.

The eval set is not a demo script. Each scenario has an expected output, a rubric score, and a trace. Adversarial cases are run deliberately to show exactly where the system breaks.

Standard

B2B SaaS onboarding feature

PM input: “churn is high in month 2.” Expected: synthesized themes from 5 sources, 3 RICE-scored options, PRD with measurable activation metric, Critic score ≥7/10.

critic_score ≥ 7.0

Researcher must distinguish “us-too” feature from differentiated response. Expected: at least 2 differentiated options in RICE output.

options_differentiated ≥ 2

Known Limitations

Where it works well.
Where it doesn’t — and why.

Disclosing failure modes is part of the submission and part of the pitch. A system that knows its limits is a system that can be trusted in production.

Works well when…
  • ✓PM provides 2+ heterogeneous feedback sources (tickets + reviews)
  • ✓Product area has some existing company docs in the RAG index
  • ✓Feature scope is clear enough to name a user segment
  • ✓B2B or consumer SaaS context (most LLM training signal)
Failure modes to disclose
  • ⚠Feedback clustering weaker than Kraftful/Enterpret on noisy production data
  • ⚠Competitor scan may hallucinate product details when sources are thin
  • ⚠RICE sizing is a PM estimate range — confidence labels are honest about this
  • ⚠Cold-start: empty RAG index produces generic recommendations until seeded
